# Lead Qualification Model

Use this model to score B2B leads consistently.

The default score is 0–100.

If the user provides their own qualification criteria, adapt the model to those criteria instead of following the default weights blindly.

## ICP Eligibility Gate

Before scoring, confirm that the prospect satisfies every material ICP boundary and exclusion supplied by the user.

An out-of-scope prospect is not a lower-scoring qualified lead. Exclude it from the qualified results and, if this creates a shortfall, report the shortfall before offering the user an optional expansion of the ICP.

## 1. Ideal Customer Profile Fit — 0 to 35 points

Evaluate how closely the company matches the target customer profile.

Consider:

- industry
- location
- company size
- business model
- customer type
- other requirements defined by the user

Scoring guide:

- 30–35: excellent match
- 20–29: good match
- 10–19: partial match
- 0–9: weak match

## 2. Evidence of Need — 0 to 30 points

Look for evidence that the company may actually need the user's product or service.

Examples:

- outdated technology
- missing functionality
- poor website or customer experience
- rapid growth
- hiring for a relevant role
- customer complaints
- expansion into a new market
- an obvious operational problem the user's offer could solve

Scoring guide:

- 25–30: strong evidence of need
- 15–24: meaningful evidence
- 5–14: weak evidence
- 0–4: no useful evidence found

Do not assume a need without evidence.

## 3. Timing and Buying Signals — 0 to 20 points

Look for signals that the company may be more likely to act now.

Examples:

- recent funding
- expansion
- new leadership
- new office or location
- new product launch
- active hiring
- recent technology changes
- public request for relevant suppliers or services

Scoring guide:

- 15–20: strong recent buying signal
- 8–14: useful signal
- 1–7: weak signal
- 0: no signal found

## 4. Reachability — 0 to 15 points

Evaluate whether there is a realistic way to contact the company.

Consider:

- relevant decision-maker identified
- verified business email
- public business phone number
- LinkedIn or another professional contact channel

Scoring guide:

- 12–15: decision-maker and reliable contact found
- 7–11: useful contact information available
- 1–6: only general company contact information
- 0: no useful contact route found

## Negative Signals

Reduce the final score when there is clear evidence that the company is a poor lead.

Examples:

- company appears inactive or closed
- already uses a solution that clearly makes the offer irrelevant
- evidence is outdated or unreliable

Prospects that are explicitly outside the target market, company-size boundary, or another mandatory ICP criterion should already have been excluded by the ICP Eligibility Gate rather than retained with a deduction.

Use judgment when applying deductions.

## Lead Priority

After calculating the score, classify the lead:

- 80–100: Priority A — strong lead
- 60–79: Priority B — promising lead
- 40–59: Priority C — possible lead
- 0–39: Low priority

## Evidence Rule

Every meaningful score should be based on observable evidence.

Clearly distinguish between:

- Verified fact
- Reasonable inference
- Unknown information

Never invent facts to increase a lead score.

When evidence is missing, mark it as unknown rather than assuming a positive result.
