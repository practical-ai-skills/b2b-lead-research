# Lead Research Output Format

Use this format when presenting researched leads to the user.

## Default Lead Table

Include these columns when the information is relevant and available:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Company | Company name |
| Website | Official company website |
| Location | City and country |
| Industry | Main business category |
| Why it matches | Short explanation of why this company fits the user's target |
| Evidence of need | Observable signals that suggest the company may need the user's product or service |
| Lead score | Score from 0 to 100 |
| Priority | A, B, C, or Low priority |
| Decision-maker | Relevant person, if reliably identified |
| Role | Decision-maker's job title |
| Business email | Verified or publicly available business email, if available |
| Contact status | Verified, unverified, general contact, or unavailable |
| Sources | Sources used to support the research |

## Evidence Labels

When presenting important information, distinguish between:

- Verified — directly supported by a reliable source
- Inferred — reasonable conclusion based on available evidence
- Unknown — information was not found

Never present inferred or unknown information as verified fact.

## Summary

Before the full lead table, provide a short summary containing:

- requested lead quantity
- exact ICP used, including material exclusions
- number of leads researched
- number of qualified exact ICP matches returned
- number of Priority A leads
- number of Priority B leads
- important patterns found
- major limitations or missing data

If the qualified result count is lower than the requested quantity, state the shortfall and the limiting ICP constraint. Do not fill the gap with adjacent categories unless the user approves a broader ICP.

## Large Lead Lists

If the user requests a large number of leads, do not reduce research quality just to reach the requested number.

Prefer:

1. a smaller batch of well-qualified leads;
2. explaining what additional data or external tools are needed;
3. continuing in batches when appropriate.

When useful, offer a CSV-compatible structure for export.
